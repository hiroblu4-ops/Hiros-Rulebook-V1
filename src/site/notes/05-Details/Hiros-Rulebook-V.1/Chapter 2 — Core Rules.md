This chapter explains the core mechanics used during play: **how to roll dice, resolve actions, resist consequences, determine Position and Effect, and track progress toward a goal**. You do not need to memorize every rule. Refer to this chapter whenever you need to determine how a roll works or what happens after one.

## 2.1 The Basic Dice System
![Pasted image 20261109232517.png\|284](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261109232517.png)

The game uses six-sided dice (d6) to resolve uncertain actions and situations. When you roll a pool of dice, roll all the dice together and use only the **highest result** to determine the outcome.

| *Highest Result* | *Outcome*            | *Meaning*                                                                               |
| ---------------- | -------------------- | --------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–3              | **Poor Result**      | Things go poorly. You may fail to achieve your goal and face a consequence.             |
| 4–5              | **Partial Success**  | You accomplish what you attempted, but a consequence or complication may arise.         |
| One 6            | **Full Success**     | You accomplish what you attempted without the usual complications of a partial success. |
| Two or more 6s   | **Critical Success** | You succeed and gain an additional advantage.                                           |
### Rolling with Zero or Fewer Dice
If you need to roll but have zero or fewer dice, roll **2d6 and take the lower result**. This makes the outcome more dangerous, but it still uses the same result bands.

*NOTE: You cannot score a Critical Success when using this fallback, even if both dice show a 6.*
### Increasing a Dice Pool
Some mechanics or circumstances may grant bonus dice. For example, a character may gain an advantage from the situation or use a specific ability that provides additional dice.

Add any applicable bonus dice to the pool you're using before rolling. Read only the highest die, considering a critical succes if there are two or more 6s.

## 2.2 Types of Rolls
![Pasted image 20261109232710.png\|294](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261109232710.png)

The game uses different roll procedures for different situations: **Action Rolls, Resistance Rolls, Fortune Rolls, Recovery Rolls, and Special Trait Rolls**. Each roll follows the aforementioned basic dice system.

[[05-Details/Hiros-Rulebook-V.1/Chapter 3 — Characters\|Chapter 3 — Characters]] explains this further more while [[Chapter 4 — Advancement\|Chapter 4 — Advancement]] elaborates how XP could also improve your dice pool and rolls.
### Action Rolls
Make an **Action Roll** when your character attempts something dangerous, troublesome, or uncertain and the outcome needs to be determined.

*To make an Action Roll:*
1. Describe what your character does and what you want to accomplish. The Host will determine whether or not you need to roll for it and could present a Position & Effect that you both need to consider.
    
2. Choose the Action that best represents your approach and roll a number of d6s equal to that Action's rating. 
    
3. Read the highest die and let it determine the outcome. 
    
4. Resolve any progress, advantages, or consequences that follow.
     
Your Action rating determines your base dice pool. A higher rating gives you more dice and more opportunities to roll a high result. Not every action requires a roll. If an action is straightforward and there is no meaningful uncertainty, the Host can simply describe what happens.
### Resistance Rolls
Make a **Resistance Roll** when your character suffers a consequence and you want to reduce its severity. Unlike an Action Roll, a Resistance Roll uses the **Attribute**, not an Action. The roll only determines how much Strain you take, not whether your Resistance works.

*To make a Resistance Roll:*
1. The Host describes the consequence.
    
2. Decide whether you want to resist it before it is applied.
    
3. Choose an Attribute: Physical or Mental and roll a number of d6s equal to that Attribute's rating. It will mitigate the consequence or reduce the severity level.
    
4. Take Strain equal to **6 minus the result of your roll**.
    
Resistance always reduces the consequence, regardless of the roll's result. It does not eliminate the consequence entirely. For example, a character who would suffer a broken leg might *resist the injury* and suffer a sprained ankle instead.
#### Critical Resistance
If your Resistance Roll scores a Critical Success (two or more 6s), you **don't take Strain at all**, and *remove one Strain from your character's track.*
#### Choosing an Attribute
When choosing the Attribute to make the resistance roll, choose the one that best fits the kind of consequence you are resisting. **Physical** for physical danger, bodily harm, or physical exertion. **Mental** for psychological pressure, intimidation, or other mental threats.

*If the appropriate Attribute is unclear, the Host determines which one applies.*

### Fortune Rolls
A **Fortune Roll** lets the Clan or Host determine an uncertain outcome without relying on a character's specific Action rating or Dice Pool. It is useful when the outcome depends on luck, circumstances, or factors outside direct control. The Host chooses the dice pool based on some logical circumstances or simply makes them up for the roll. However, fortune Rolls are still based on the *result bands of the Basic Dice System*.

The Host will interpret what the result's outcome is for the question being answered or situation rolled for. Their narrative meaning depends on the circumstances. Setting a Position & Effect can further guide the Host's interpretation when relevant.

Fortune Rolls can also contribute to *Progress Tracks* when the Host determines that the circumstances warrant it.

### Recovery Rolls
A **Recovery Roll** is used to recover from injuries. It advances the Recovery track, and completing that track reduces the severity level of all of your injuries by one level. You can recover through rest, medical treatment, help from other characters, or other appropriate narrative opportunities. Recovery may happen preferably during Downtime or during any RP when circumstances allow it. 

*To make a Recovery Roll:*
1. Determine who is providing the treatment or attempting the recovery.
    
2. Use the appropriate **Finesse Action or Special Trait dice pool** based on the character providing the healing.
    
3. Roll the dice and read the highest result.
    
4. Mark Recovery progress according to the result.


*Additional rules when determining the dice pool for a recovery roll:*
- **Another character provides treatment:** Use the appropriate Finesse Action or Special Trait(Specifically a healing Trait) dice pool of the character providing the healing.
    
- **An NPC with medical experience provides treatment:** Use a standard dice pool of **2 dice**.
    
- **You treat yourself:** Use your own appropriate dice pool for the Finesse Action. However, you may suffer **1 Strain** to attempt the Recovery Roll.
    
- **You recover in a dangerous situation:** Reduce the Recovery Roll by one die.
#### Recovery Progress
The Recovery track has four tick boxes. Mark progress according to the highest result rolled. When all four ticks are checked, **each of your injuries decreases by one severity level**. Once healing takes effect, clear the Recovery track so it can be filled again through future recovery attempts.

| *Roll Result*  | *Recovery Progress* |
| -------------- | ------------------- |
| 1–3            | 1 tick              |
| 4–5            | 2 ticks             |
| One 6          | 3 ticks             |
| Two or more 6s | 5 ticks             |

*NOTE: A Critical Success grants 5 ticks, even though the Recovery track has only four boxes. Complete the track, clear it and let the extra tick carry over onto the fresh Recovery track.*

### Special Trait Rolls
A **Special Trait** is a defining ability or feature that makes your character mechanically distinct. Its description determines what it can do and how you use it.

Note that Special Traits do not all use the same roll procedure. The kind of roll depends on the trait's function. **Action-based Special Traits use the Action Roll procedure.** For example, a trait that lets a character perform an extraordinary feat of strength or move with exceptional speed requires an Action Roll. **Attribute-based Special Traits use the Resistance Roll procedure, including its Strain cost.** For example, a trait that lets a character endure extreme pain or resist mental influence requires a Resistance Roll.

The trait's description determines which procedure applies, you are able to write that down. If the trait's use or description is unclear or complex, seek advice and discuss it with a Host or High Rank.
#### Special Trait Dice Pool
Your Special Trait level determines the number of dice you roll when using it. Refer to the table below.

| *Special Trait Level* | *Dice Pool* |
| --------------------- | ----------- |
| Level 1               | 1 die       |
| Level 2               | 2 dice      |
| Level 3               | 3 dice      |
| Level 4               | 4 dice      |
#### Special Trait Limit & Advancement
You can use your Special Trait **only once during an Operation or any kind of event**. Each Special Trait has its own eight-box XP track. When all eight boxes are filled, you can mark one additional level tickbox to advance the trait. Increasing its level improves and develops the trait, adding one more dice to it's dice pool.

For reference, check [[05-Details/Hiros-Rulebook-V.1/Chapter 3 — Characters\|Chapter 3 — Characters]] & [[Chapter 4 — Advancement\|Chapter 4 — Advancement]]

## 2.3 Consequences
![Pasted image 20261109233045.png\|305](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261109233045.png)

A **consequence** is an adverse outcome that arises from a dangerous, incomplete, or unsuccessful action. Consequences make actions matter. They can introduce new problems, increase danger, or force characters to make difficult choices.

*Examples of consequences include:*
- Suffering an injury.
- Losing time or an opportunity.
- Attracting unwanted attention.
- Losing resources or damaging an asset.
- Making a faction or character hostile toward you or the Clan.
- Creating a new problem that must be dealt with.
    
A consequence does not always mean an action has completely failed. A character can accomplish what they intended while still suffering a complication.
### When Consequences Occur
A Poor Result means things go poorly. The intended goal may not be achieved, and the Host may introduce a *consequence*. A Partial Success means the character accomplishes what they attempted, but a consequence or complication may arise. 

A Full Success means the character accomplishes what they attempted without a consequence, while a Critical Success provides no consequences and maybe even an additional advantage. The Host describes the outcome in the context of the action and the current situation.
### Resisting a Consequence
When the Host presents a consequence, you decide whether to resist it **before it is applied**. The Host always allows you to resist.

*If you choose to resist:*
1. Make a Resistance Roll using the appropriate Attribute.
    
2. Pay the Strain cost determined by the roll.
    
3. Reduce the severity of the consequence.
    
Resistance always works to reduce the consequence, but it never removes the consequence entirely. For example, if an attack would give your character a broken leg, resisting might reduce it to a sprained ankle. The character still suffers an injury, but it is less severe.

The Host determines the reduced consequence based on the original consequence and the circumstances. Trust your Gut.

## 2.4 Position and Effect
![Pasted image 20261109232818.png\|288](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261109232818.png)

Position and Effect are optional guidelines that help clarify the danger and potential impact of an action. **Position** describes how dangerous the character's situation is, while **Effect** describes how much the character can accomplish if the action succeeds.

 The Host can set them when the situation needs clarification or when a player asks for them for further insight in whatever they're gonna attempt. They do not need to be assigned to every Action Roll and is used as more of a guide. Note that these are separate considerations. An action can be dangerous but highly effective, or relatively safe but limited in what it can achieve. 
 
 *"If you are not willing to risk the usual you will have to settle for the ordinary"*
### Position
Worse Position means more severe potential consequences. Position helps the Host and Players judge the danger of the situation and the severity of possible consequences. This applies whether the roll is a *Poor Result, Partial Success, Full Success, or Critical Success.*

| *Position*     | *Meaning*                                                                                   |
| -------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **Controlled** | The situation is relatively safe, and the character has room to recover if things go wrong. |
| **Risky**      | There is meaningful danger or a possibility of complications.                               |
| **Desperate**  | The character is in a dangerous situation where failure could have serious consequences.    |
*NOTE: Every Action Roll made from a Desperate Position grants **1 XP**, regardless of the roll's result.*
### Effect
Effect describes how much the action can accomplish. Effect can influence how much progress an Action Roll narratively makes. The effect will only vary on intensity and can either be good or bad. *"With great risk comes great reward"*

| *Effect*     | *Meaning*                                    |
| ------------ | -------------------------------------------- |
| **Limited**  | The action accomplishes less than expected.  |
| **Standard** | The action accomplishes the expected amount. |
| **Great**    | The action accomplishes more than expected.  |

## 2.5 Progress Tracks
![Pasted image 20261109233235.png\|299](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261109233235.png)

A **Progress Track** represents progress toward a goal or the development of a situation over time. A track is divided into tick boxes. As characters or the Clan makes meaningful progress, ticks are marked until the track is complete and what it represented narratively happens. For example, if a track represents finding the location of a missing person, completing it means the group achieves that objective.

 On the other hand, the track can be used against Players. It could measure how close a **ticking time bomb, an approaching threat, or a looming catastrophe** could occur. Instead of filling boxes to succeed, the track fills up to represent failure, destruction, or the enemy gaining the upper hand.

*Progress Tracks can represent:*
- Completing a project.
- Investigating a mystery.
- Overcoming a difficult obstacle.
- Developing a threat.
- Advancing a faction's plans.
- How long it takes for a guard to notice the Clan.
    
Not every task needs a Progress Track. If a single action can resolve a task, a track may be unnecessary.
*NOTE: Bad rolls fill up the Tracks against players, and Good ones towards a certain goal can fill up the Good Tracks.*
### Choosing a Track Size
The number of tick boxes indicates how much progress a goal requires. The following track sizes are guidelines. The Host and players can use them to represent the scale of the objective.

| *Track Size*    | *Suggested Use*                        |
| --------------- | -------------------------------------- |
| **4 ticks**     | A simple or short-term goal.           |
| **6 ticks**     | A goal requiring several steps.        |
| **8 ticks**     | A difficult or long-term goal.         |
| **10–12 ticks** | A complex goal with several obstacles. |
*NOTE: Tracks can be represented by emojis during an event. For example, 🟩🟩🟩🟥🟥🟥*
### Creating a Progress Track
Players or the Host can communicate together, initiate, and decide when a goal or situation needs a Progress Track. Create one when the objective cannot be resolved meaningfully with a single action and needs to develop over multiple actions or scenes.

*When creating a track, establish:* 
- The name of the track, which should be clear enough that everyone understands what progress means.
- The track's required amount of ticks to be filled.
- The outcome or what happens when the track is complete.
*NOTE: When the track is no longer relevant, it's alright to erase it!*
### Marking Progress
Either a roll or the effect of a roll contributes to the progression of a track, mark ticks based on the effect level or roll result. If an action would mark more ticks than remain on a track, mark only the remaining tick boxes. Do not carry excess ticks over to another track. Then, once the track is full, resolve its outcome.

| *Effect Level* | *Roll Result*                     | *Progress* |
| -------------- | --------------------------------- | ---------- |
| Limited        | Poor Result (1–3)                 | 1 tick     |
| Standard       | Partial Success (4–5)             | 2 ticks    |
| Great          | Full Success (one 6)              | 3 ticks    |
|                | Critical Success (two or more 6s) | 5 ticks    |
