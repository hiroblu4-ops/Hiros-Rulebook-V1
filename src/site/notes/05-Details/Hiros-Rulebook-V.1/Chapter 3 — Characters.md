## 3.1 Character Basics
Characters are the people you play. Each has their own abilities, resources, and limits. These determine what they can accomplish, how they handle danger, and how they could develop over time.

Your character sheet records the information you need during play. You do not need to memorize every rule; use this chapter as a reference whenever you need it.

Every character and their sheet has the following features:

- ***Personal***
    - **Name:** Your character's name.
    - **Notes:** Are there to record other details as the story progresses.
      
- ***Core Mechanics***
    - **Special Trait:** An optional defining ability unique to your character.
    - **Levels:** Tracks the advancement of your Special Trait.
    - **Attributes:** Allows characters to make *Resistance Rolls*. It has its own XP track.
    - **Actions:** Allows characters to make *Action Rolls* based on its rating, which can be advanced by filling an Attribute's XP track.
      
- ***Health & Stress***
    - **Strain:** Is the pressure your character endures.
    - **Trauma:** Represents the permanent consequences of accumulated Strain.
    - **Injuries:** Are a character's physical harm and its *Severity*.
      
- ***Economy & Inventory***
    - **Credit and Stash:** Tracks your character's wealth and stored resources.
    - **Gear:** Determines how many abstract items your character can carry.

## 3.2 Attributes and Actions
Characters have two Attributes: **Physical** and **Mental**. Each Attribute is associated with three Actions.

Attributes and Actions serve different purposes:
- *Attributes* determine the dice rolled when resisting consequences.
- *Actions* determine the dice rolled when attempting something.
- *Attribute XP tracks* record progress toward improving an Attribute and its associated Actions.
    
 Each Action has its own *Rating*, represented by the marked boxes in its row on your character sheet. while both Attribute's rating is represented by the first column of all actions, while the six boxes placed on the right beside the Attribute's name represent that Attribute's XP track.
 
![Pasted image 20261108170725.png\|323](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108170725.png)
### Physical
The **Physical Attribute** represents your character's ability to handle physical acts and resist physical danger, exertion, and bodily harm. It is associated with three Actions: *Force*, *Maneuver*, and *Finesse*.
#### Force
**Force** is the ability to apply physical power directly. Use Force when you need to overpower an opponent, break something, hold something in place, or accomplish a task through raw strength.

*Examples:*
- Forcing open a locked door.
- Breaking a crate open.
- Holding up a collapsing structure long enough for someone to escape.
#### Maneuver
**Maneuver** is the ability to move quickly, navigate danger, and control your body's movement. Use Maneuver whenever rolling for speed, agility, coordination, or precise movement through a dangerous environment is relevant.

*Examples:*
- Leaping between rooftops.
- Dodging an incoming attack.
- Navigating a hazardous obstacle course.
#### Finesse
**Finesse** is the ability to perform delicate, precise, or controlled physical actions. Use Finesse when success depends on accuracy, careful handling, or subtle physical movements. It can even be used to create a physical distraction or other related actions.

*Examples:*
- Picking a pocket.
- Disarming a mechanical trap.
- Creating a tool.
  
### Mental
The **Mental Attribute** represents your character's ability to act on and resist psychological, social, and other mental elements. Mental is associated with three Actions: *Perceive*, *Influence*, and *Deceive*.
#### Perceive
**Perceive** is the ability to notice details, interpret your surroundings, and recognize things that others might overlook. Use Perceive when you need to observe, investigate, detect danger, or make sense of what is happening around you.

*Examples:*
- Spotting an ambush before it happens.
- Noticing that a room has been searched.
- Finding a clue to a mystery.
#### Influence
**Influence** is the ability to affect other people through your presence, words, authority, or emotional appeal. Use Influence when you need to persuade, command, reassure, negotiate with, or otherwise sway someone.

*Examples:*
- Convincing a guard to let you pass.
- Negotiating a deal with a rival.
- Rallying frightened allies to keep fighting.
#### Deceive
**Deceive** is the ability to mislead others and conceal the truth. Use Deceive when you need to lie convincingly, disguise your intentions, create a false impression, or manipulate what someone believes.

*Examples:*
- Convincing a patron that you're invited when you're actually banned from Lonely Note.
- Trying to fool someone by hiding your real intentions during a negotiation.
- Creating a convincing distraction to draw attention away from your allies.
  
### Choosing an Action
No need to ask for permission when you attempt something risky or uncertain, unless you believe it's truly necessary. Roleplay normally and describe what your character does. When the Host determines if you need to roll for it and sets up a *Position & Effect* for your roll (optional), choose a fitting action and make the roll based on it's rating.

*Example of Play:*
> ***Player:*** 
> Shintaro doesn’t waste time panicking. He spits his half-burned cigarette onto the floor and lunges across the room, slamming his weight against the heavy mahogany desk to slide it directly in front of the shaking door. He then wraps his leather jacket around his forearm and drives his fist straight down into the locked glass drawer beneath the desk, aiming to shatter it and snatch the drive.
> 
> ***Host:***
> The desk groans as a heavy boot thuds against the other side of the door, bowing the wood inward. Sparks fly from the broken neon sign outside the window, casting jagged shadows across the room. This is incredibly tense. I'm setting this at a ***Desperate* Position**. those goons are a second away from bursting through. However, your **Effect** is ***Great***. If you pull this off, you'll smash through the drawer completely and grab that drive.
> 
> *What action are you rolling?*
> 
> ***Player:***
> I’m rolling ***Force*** since Shintaro's destroying property to get what he wants. He has 2 marked ticks in Force, so I roll two dice.
> ...

Refer to [[05-Details/Hiros-Rulebook-V.1/Chapter 2 — Core Rules\|Chapter 2 — Core Rules]] for further information.

### Making a Resistance Roll
When you resist a consequence, use the appropriate *Attribute* (Mental for resisting mentally and Physical for resisting Physically) and base the roll on it's rating. Resisting costs 6 Strain minus the result of your roll and reduces the severity of the consequence. However, it does not eliminate the conseqeunce.

Your Physical and Mental ratings determine the dice you roll when resisting the corresponding kinds of consequences. When you're not sure or the situation is obscure, the Host determines which Attribute would apply to the threat you are resisting.

*Example:*
- A goon stabs Shintaro's side, if his Player wants to reduce the kind of severe level 3 Injury that attack gives Shintaro, he would have to make a roll using Shintaro's **Physical** Attribute. The roll always succeeds but the Injury's severity level will be reduced accordingly.
- A rival tries to break Shintaro's resolve during a brutal interrogation, torturing him to make him reveal the clan's safehouse. If Shintaro's player wants to reduce the Strain this costs, they must make a roll using his **Mental** Attribute.
  
*NOTE: Dice pool is the amount of dice you have to roll to attempt something. Dice Pool = Rating*
### Attribute XP and Improvement
![Pasted image 20261108110821.png\|304](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108110821.png)    ![Pasted image 20261108110830.png\|301](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108110830.png)

Each Attribute has its own **XP track**, represented by the boxes beside its name on your character sheet. This track records your progress toward improving that Attribute. When the track is filled, the Attribute advances, allowing you to mark one additional tick in an Action associated with it.

For example, advancing Physical allows you to add one tick to Force, Maneuver, or Finesse. Advancing Mental allows you to add one tick to Perceive, Influence, or Deceive.

The XP track and the Action Ratings serve different purposes: the track measures progress toward advancement, while the Action ratings determine the dice you roll. Advancing an Attribute and gaining one additional tick means an improved *Rating*, an improved Rating means your *Dice Pool* for that advanced Action or Attribute gets one more dice at default.

[[Chapter 4 — Advancement\|Chapter 4 — Advancement]] explains how characters earn XP, fill these tracks, and improve their ratings.

## 3.3 Strain and Trauma
**Strain** represents the pressure your character endures while *Exerting themselves*, *Resisting consequences*, proposing*Flashbacks*, using their *Special Trait*, *Recovering on their own*, or other Special Actions and activities.

When your Strain track fills, you gain one permanent **Trauma**.  Trauma represents a lasting consequence of the pressure your character has endured. Please define or write down the Trauma your character suffers.

*Trauma References for Unsure Players:*
Your Character becomes...
- **Cold:** Unmoved by emotions or social ties.
- **Haunted:** Prone to flashbacks, hallucinations, or reverie.
- **Obsessed:** Fixated on a specific person, activity, or ideology.
- **Paranoid:** Unable to trust others and sees danger everywhere.
- **Reckless:** Disregards personal safety and best interests.
- **Soft:** Becomes sentimental, gentle, or passive.
- **Unstable:** Experiences volatile shifts to rage, despair, or impulse.
- **Vicious:** Seeks out opportunities to hurt people without reason.

For more details on how those aforementioned Special Actions work, check [[Chapter 5 — Strain, Trauma & Special Actions\|Chapter 5 — Strain, Trauma & Special Actions]]

## 3.4 Injuries and Recovery
Injuries represent physical harm. Each Injury has a severity level, from Level 1 to Level 4. Higher levels represent more severe harm, while Level 4 is fatal or causes catastrophic consequences.

Your character can sustain up to five Injuries at a time: (Kinda graphic, read at your own discretion.)
- Level 1 injuries have 2 available slots.
- Level 2 injuries have 2 available slots.
- Level 3 injuries have only one available slot.

| *Injury Type* | *Examples*                                                               | *In-game effect*                                                                                                                     |
| ------------- | ------------------------------------------------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------ |
| Level <br>1   | Bruised ribs, Sprained wrist, Black eye, Sore shoulder.                  | Whatever you attempt will have a Lesser Effect.                                                                                      |
| Level 2       | Deep slash to the arm, Concussion, Stab wound to the thigh, Broken nose. | Any Action Roll you make is reduced by one die.                                                                                      |
| Level 3       | Severed tendon, Shattered kneecap, Third-degree burns, Broken Femur.     | Your character cannot perform any standard action rolls on their own unless they *Exert* themselves or are helped by another Player. |
### Suffering an Injury
Suffering an injury occurs when you face a consequence from a failed or risky roll, or an enemy attack. When your character suffers an Injury, record it at the appropriate severity level that the Host provides.

![Pasted image 20261108113058.png\|257](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108113058.png)

If you suffer an injury of a certain level, you must write it in an open slot of that level. If all slots at that specific level are already filled, the new injury "*rolls up*" to the next highest open slot. You do not have to accept the injury as given. Before an injury is written on your sheet, you can tell the Host that you want to Resist it, which can reduce the severity level of that injury.
### Recovering from Injuries
Characters can recover from injuries through rest, medical treatment, help from others, or other appropriate methods with a **Recovery Roll**. These opportunities might include anything from receiving treatment from an NPC or another character, using a medical cryostasis machine, to simply finding time to recover during RP.

You can also attempt recovery on your own. When no other opportunity is available, you may suffer **1 Strain** to make a Recovery Roll on your own. Recovery takes time, and dangerous situations may limit your opportunities to heal. Downtime provides a safer opportunity to recover without the constant threat of enemy factions.

#### Making a Recovery Roll
To recover, make a Recovery Roll using the **Finesse Action** or **Special Trait** dice pool determined by the character providing the healing. If it's an NPC with Medical Experience healing your character, they have a standard of **2** in their Dice Pool. If you are treating yourself, use your own appropriate dice pool to make the roll. Trying to recover in a dangerous situation reduces the roll by one die.

The Recovery track has four tick boxes arranged in a square. 
![Pasted image 20261108110539.png\|279](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108110539.png)
Fill the boxes according to the highest result rolled:

| **Roll result** | **Recovery progress** |
| --------------- | --------------------- |
| 1–3             | 1 tick                |
| 4–5             | 2 ticks               |
| One 6           | 3 ticks               |
| Two or more 6s  | 5 ticks               |
When all four ticks are checked, **each of your Injuries decreases by one severity level**. For example, a Level 3 Injury becomes Level 2, a Level 2 Injury becomes Level 1 and so on. Once healing takes effect, clear the Recovery track so it can be filled again through future recovery attempts.

## 3.5 Special Traits and Advancement
Every player can optionally define or provide a **Special Trait** for their character. A Special Trait is a defining ability or feature that makes them distinct. Special Traits may provide unique abilities or change how a character approaches particular situations. Their individual descriptions explain what they do.

Each Special Trait has its own XP track, represented by the smaller eight boxes on the character sheet.
![Pasted image 20261108111000.png\|234](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108111000.png)
This track records progress toward advancing the Trait. When all eight boxes are filled, you get to mark one more of the larger boxes next to it, letting the Special Trait level up. Increasing its Level improves and develops the Trait.

The way a Trait is rolled for depends on what it does and how the player defined it. Action-based Traits use Action Rolls, and Attribute-based Traits use Resistance Rolls where the cost is Strain. The amount of Dice you need to roll for the usage of a Special Trait is determined by it's level, where level 1 = 1 Die, Level 2 = 2 Dice, and so on. However, you can only use your Special Trait once during an Operation.

*NOTE: The Special Trait XP track is separate from the Attribute XP tracks. You have to spend XP separately. Filling an Attribute's track improves the rating of the Action or Attribute. On the other hand, filling a Special Trait's XP track improves the Level and Rating of the Special Trait. Complicated, innit?*

[[Chapter 4 — Advancement\|Chapter 4 — Advancement]] explains how characters earn XP, to fill their Special Trait track, and advance their Traits.

## 3.6 Credit and Stash
Credit represents your character's available wealth and ability to pay for significant expenses, it is the abstract measurement of a character's cash and liquid assets, while Stash represents wealth set aside for later use.

Credit is measured using the Credit units. Every character has a limit of 4 on-hand credits, which is the amount of credits a character can have available. Every character also has 40 units of stash with 4 ratings that dictate how wealthy their character's lifestyle is. 

| *Credit Tier*  | *Financial Milestone*          | *Examples*                                                                                        |
| -------------- | ------------------------------ | ------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| **1 Credit**   | Standard Monthly Income        | A thick envelope of cash, a month's rent for a premium city apartment, or top-tier personal tech. |
| **2 Credits**  | Small Business Weekly Earnings | A professional tactical firearm, high-end designer clothing, or authentic collectible art.        |
| **4 Credits**  | Heavy Cash Shipment            | An incredibly rare diamond ring, or a significant down payment on a brand-new vehicle.            |
| **6 Credits**  | Small Business Monthly Profits | A wall safe packed with bullion and bonds, or a heavily restricted black-market artifact.         |
| **8 Credits**  | Annual Profit                  | A high-end luxury vehicle, a commercial transit truck, or the deed to a small suburban property.  |
| **10 Credits** | Life-Changing Wealth           | A secured bank vault haul, a massive corporate bribe, or a permanent retirement stash.            |
Any excess unit Credit must be spent or put in your character's Stash. Note that removing Stash to convert it into Credit results in a 2:1 loss: you must remove 2 Stash to receive 1 Credit. Fortunately, Credit can be added directly to your Stash with an equal conversion rate.

Refer to [[Chapter 4 — Advancement\|Chapter 4 — Advancement]] for more details on spending, storing, and managing wealth.

## 3.7 Gear and Load
Gear represents the equipment your character brings into an operation, it also determines how many abstract items your character can carry without listing every minor object individually.

Abstract items are useful possessions that do not need to be recorded individually in your inventory with the inventory bot. They can be introduced later during the action when characters need them to help overcome an obstacle.

Before an operation, characters must choose their appropriate gear:
![Pasted image 20261108112927.png\|266](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108112927.png)

| *Gear Level* | *Gear Limit* |
| ------------ | ------------ |
| Light        | 2            |
| Standard     | 4            |
| Heavy        | 6            |
Using your Gear remains fair as long as the total number of abstract items that your character uses does not exceed your chosen Gear Limit. Higher Gear Levels also dictate your character's narrative profile, making you faster and stealthier at a Light Gear Level, or slower and more conspicuous as an operative at Heavy Level.

## 3.8 Default Character Details
 A newly created character begins with the following technical details:
- **Action Ratings:** You have 4 ticks to distribute among your six Actions, with no more than 2 ticks in a single Action.
    
- **Attribute XP:** Both Attribute XP tracks begin empty.
    
- **Special Trait:** You are optionally able to write your own Special Trait, provided that the Host approved of it. Its eight-box XP track begins empty, but the Level of your Special trait begins at 1.
    
- **Strain & Trauma:** You begin with an empty Strain track and no Trauma unless you propose it.
    
- **Injuries:** You have no Injuries and nothing to Recover.
    
- **Credit and Stash:** Your character is limited to have not more than 2 Credits on-hand and lesser than 10 Credits in their stash unless there is a considerable reason.
