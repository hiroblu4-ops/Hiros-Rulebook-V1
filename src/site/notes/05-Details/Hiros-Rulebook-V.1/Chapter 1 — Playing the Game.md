Play follows three phases: **Freeplay, Operation, and Downtime**. Together, these phases form the game's main cycle and gives every kind of event a purpose. The Clan explores opportunities, undertakes dangerous work, deals with the consequences, and prepares for what comes next.

Freeplay creates opportunities. Operations put characters to the test. Downtime allows characters to recover, pursue goals, and prepare for what comes next. Any of these phases can be hosted with any type of Operation, whether it be a Main or Mini Event.

These phases do not have to happen in a single sitting. Play can be divided into smaller segments and continued later. For asynchronous play, the Host and players can leave a scene at a suitable stopping point or cliffhanger.

## 1.1 Freeplay
![Pasted image 20261108165024.png\|223](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108165024.png)

**Freeplay** is the open-ended phase of the game. Characters can roleplay, interact with others, pursue personal goals, gather information, recover, indulge, and explore opportunities. Players have considerable freedom during Freeplay. They decide what their characters want to do and communicate their intentions to the Host when necessary.
*NOTE: Communication is very important. If you want to do something for your Character or The Clan's Development, you are free to propose it to the Host or other Players.*
### Gathering Information
During Freeplay, the Clan can choose to *gather information* if they wish to discover leads, identify targets, learn about potential threats, or uncover opportunities for an *Operation*. The Host can present jobs, opportunities, and possible targets in any creative way that suits them. Players may pursue these leads or propose their own goals and plans.

Freeplay continues until the Clan is ready to begin an Operation. The Clan can return to Freeplay after *Downtime*, which is Freeplay but for the aftermath of an Operation.

## 1.2 Preparing an Operation
*Before* an Operation begins, the players use the information they have gathered from Freeplay to decide what they want to accomplish and how they intend to do it. 

Unless... they don't wanna plan at all... That's fine too, I guess. 

If the Clan needs help forming the starting plan, one or more people who want to take initiative can choose a plan type to kick off with, and provide a brief detail explaining their approach. Nothing has to be fully fleshed out, the Clan only needs to know how to initiate the plan, every other detail will be made up following the start. 
*NOTE: Before it begins, make sure that every character has chosen their Gear for the amount of abstract items they'll be able to use during the operation.*
### Kick Off Plan Types
- **Assault:** A strategy that uses direct force or violence to achieve the objective.
- **Deception:** It relies on trickery, disguises, or false pretenses.
- **Stealth:** For schemes that requires sneaking past defenses without attracting attention.
- **Social:** Here, characters leverage relationships, persuasion, or manipulation.
     
These plan types help establish the Clan's intended approach as well as help the other players understand what to primarily do for the Operation. They do not restrict what the players can attempt once the Operation begins.
### The Clan's Position Roll
Once the plan is established, the one who takes initiative (usually the leader or a High-Ranking member) rolls one six-sided die to determine the Clan's starting circumstances.
*TIP: Spend one Credit to gain one dice for your roll and improve your circumstances.*

| *Result* | *Starting circumstances*                                                                                    |
| -------- | ----------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------------- |
| 1–3      | **Poor Position:** Things begin badly, putting the Clan at a disadvantage.                                  |
| 4–5      | **Partially Good Position:** The Clan begins from a stable position, but obstacles or complications remain. |
| 6        | **Good Position:** The Clan begins favorably, with an advantage in its circumstances.                       |
The Host uses the result to establish the opening situation and describe what the Clan encounters first and foremost.

## 1.3 During an Operation
![Pasted image 20261108164458.png\|268](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108164458.png)

An Operation is *the* main event: the Clan attempts to accomplish its objective while dealing with danger, opposition, and unexpected complications.
### The Flow of Play
1. **The Host describes the situation.** The opening situation reflects the Clan's Position roll and established plan, While following situations describe what the Clan experiences as the Operation progresses,
    
2. **Players describe their actions.** Each player must explain what their character does and what they hope to accomplish in a situation.
    
3. **The Host calls for rolls when needed.** Risky or uncertain actions are usually what rolls are made for more insight, the Host can set the Position & Effect. Aside from making Action and Resistance rolls, players can make Recovery Rolls, roll with their Special Trait, spend strain to Exert themselves, or propose Flashbacks.
    
4. **The Host describes the outcome.** Success advances the situation; failure or partial success may introduce consequences. The aformention Position & Effect help determine the danger and impact of an action when relevant.
    
5. **Players respond to consequences.** Characters may resist consequences, or react.
    
6. **The situation develops.** The Host describes new circumstances, and the players decide what to do next.
    
Injuries, lost resources, unwanted attention, and other complications can arise during an Operation but will be summed out and advanced/reduced during the aftermath or Downtime. The characters' choices shape how the events unfold.

Refer to [[05-Details/Hiros-Rulebook-V.1/Chapter 2 — Core Rules\|Chapter 2 — Core Rules]] for more information about how Mechanics work.
### Ending an Operation
An Operation can end when the Clan accomplishes its objective, when the situation reaches a natural conclusion, or when the players decide to abandon the Operation and flee. The Host helps determine when the objective has been resolved, but players also have a say in recognizing when they have achieved what they set out to do.

Abandoning an Operation does not erase its consequences. Based on how the Operation unfolded, the Host may introduce further consequences or award appropriate rewards before the group proceeds to Downtime.

## 1.4 Downtime
![Pasted image 20261108164629.png\|272](/img/user/Z_Assets/Pasted%20image%2020261108164629.png)

Downtime is the phase after an Operation, when characters and the Clan deal with the aftermath and prepare for future events. The Host establishes the immediate consequences and any relevant *Entanglements*, which are complications that might have happened during the Operation as the cost of doing underworld business. 

Characters can then pursue activities to recover, manage resources, count their rewards and gain advantages for future Operations. 
### Downtime Activities
Each character has **two Core Activities** by default after an Operation. Players decide what their characters want to do or accomplish and describe how they do it. The Host determines whether a roll is required. These activities can be used for personal goals or to benefit the Clan. Communicate with the Host to define or play your two Core Activities.

*Possible Core Activities include:*
- **Recovering:** Treat Injuries and work toward healing. This requires a Recovery Roll.
    
- **Introducing or Making a move on a Long-Term Project:** Make progress toward a goal that requires time and effort. This requires an Action roll, and based on the results, will fill a certain amount of ticks in it's Progression Track.
    
- **Acquire Asset:** Obtain useful resources, equipment, or assistance. This requires Credit.
    
- **Indulge:** Relieve accumulated Strain through a chosen relaxing vice or activity. This requires you to roll with your lowest Attribute's Dice Pool and reducing the amount of Strain by the result.
    
These are common options, not an exhaustive list. Players can propose other activities that fit their characters' goals and the circumstances, subject to the Host's approval.

Check [[05-Details/Hiros-Rulebook-V.1/Chapter 2 — Core Rules\|Chapter 2 — Core Rules]] for more insight.
### Additional Downtime Activities
After using their two free activities, a character can spend **1 Credit per Additional Core Activity**. This allows players to take on more activities when they need extra time to recover, pursue projects, or help the Clan prepare for its next Operation.

Each additional activity costs 1 Credit, regardless of whether it benefits the character personally or the Clan.

After all activities have been resolved *with or without fully roleplaying it*, the group can return to Freeplay and begin working toward its next Operation.

## 1.5 The Cycle Continues
The cycle continues as long as the Clan has ambitions to pursue and challenges to face.

Remember that play does not have to stop at the end of a phase. You can divide an Operation or other scene into smaller segments, resolve part of it, and continue later. For asynchronous play, a cliffhanger can provide a natural point to pause while leaving the group with something to look forward to.
